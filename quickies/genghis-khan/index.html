Genghis Khan

His father was poisoned when he was eight, his tribe abandoned the family, and his wife was kidnapped. He answered by building the largest contiguous land empire the world had ever seen.

4 min read · 10 key facts · 10 terms decoded

Abandoned at Eight

He was not born Genghis Khan. He was born Temüjin, sometime between 1155 and 1167, eldest child of Yesugei, a Mongol chieftain of the Borjigin clan, and his wife Hö'elün.

When Temüjin was eight, his father died and the family was abandoned by its tribe — left on the steppe in near-poverty. In those circumstances he killed his older half-brother Behter to secure his position within the family, a detail the sources do not soften.

What pulled him out was not force but personality. His charisma attracted his first followers and won him alliances with two prominent steppe leaders, Jamukha and Toghrul. Together they rescued his newlywed wife Börte after she was kidnapped by raiders. That rescue is the hinge of the early story: a destitute outcast who could persuade powerful men to fight for him.

Defeated, Vanished, Returned

The alliances did not hold. As Temüjin's reputation grew, his relationship with Jamukha soured into open warfare, and around 1187 Temüjin was badly defeated.

Then he disappears from the record. He may have spent the following years as a subject of the Jin dynasty — a comedown so complete that a man who would later conquer northern China possibly spent a decade as somebody's subject.

He re-emerged in 1196 and gained power fast. Toghrul, his old ally, came to see him as a threat and launched a surprise attack in 1203. Temüjin regrouped, overpowered Toghrul, then defeated the Naiman tribe and executed Jamukha. That left him the sole ruler of the Mongolian steppe. The whole arc — betrayal, crushing defeat, years of obscurity, return — occupies about sixteen years.

A Meritocracy Built on the Steppe

At an assembly in 1206, Temüjin formally took the title Genghis Khan, whose meaning remains uncertain.

His next move mattered more than any battle. He carried out reforms designed for long-term stability, transforming the Mongols' tribal structure into an integrated meritocracy dedicated to serving the ruling family. Loyalty and ability outranked birth and clan — which meant a talented nobody could rise, and a hostile tribe's warriors could be absorbed rather than merely defeated.

After thwarting a coup attempt by a powerful shaman, Genghis consolidated his power completely. He believed the shamanic supreme deity Tengri had destined him for world domination, and he welcomed advice from diverse sources in pursuit of it. The army he built was not a horde of raiders but a machine that could absorb the people it conquered and turn them into more of itself.

Twenty Years, Half a Continent

The campaigns came in rapid succession. In 1209 he raided the neighbouring Western Xia, who accepted Mongol terms the following year. He then attacked the Jin dynasty in a four-year campaign that ended in 1215 with the capture of the Jin capital, Zhongdu.

His general Jebe annexed the Central Asian state of Qara Khitai in 1218. The following year, after his envoys were executed, Genghis invaded the Khwarazmian Empire. That campaign toppled the Khwarazmian state and devastated Transoxiana and Khorasan, while Jebe and Subutai led an expedition that reached Georgia and Kievan Rus'.

Genghis died in 1227, on 18 or 25 August, while subduing the rebellious Western Xia. After a two-year interregnum, his third son and heir Ögedei took the throne in 1229.

Tyrant, Founder, or Both

Genghis Khan remains genuinely contested, and the contradictions are not resolvable by picking a side.

He was generous and intensely loyal to his own followers, and ruthless toward his enemies. The Mongol army under his command killed millions of people. The same conquests opened unprecedented commercial and cultural exchange across an enormous geographical area — the two facts are inseparable, produced by the same campaigns.

How he is remembered depends heavily on where you ask. In Russia and the Arab world he is a backward, savage tyrant. Recent Western scholarship has begun reassessing its older view of him as a barbarian warlord. In Mongolia he was posthumously deified, and modern Mongolians recognise him as the founding father of their nation. Few figures in history are simultaneously a national founder and a synonym for devastation.

10 key facts

  1. Genghis Khan was born Temüjin between 1155 and 1167 and was abandoned with his family by their tribe when he was eight.
  2. He killed his older half-brother Behter to secure his position in the family after his father's death.
  3. His first major alliances were formed to rescue his newlywed wife Börte, who had been kidnapped by raiders.
  4. After a bad defeat around 1187, he may have spent years as a subject of the Jin dynasty before re-emerging in 1196.
  5. He formally adopted the title Genghis Khan at an assembly in 1206, and the meaning of the title is still uncertain.
  6. He replaced the Mongols' tribal structure with an integrated meritocracy loyal to the ruling family.
  7. His four-year campaign against the Jin dynasty ended in 1215 with the capture of their capital Zhongdu.
  8. He invaded the Khwarazmian Empire in 1219 after his envoys were executed, and his generals reached Georgia and Kievan Rus'.
  9. He died in 1227 while subduing the Western Xia, and his son Ögedei took the throne after a two-year interregnum.
  10. He is remembered as a savage tyrant in Russia and the Arab world, and as a deified founding father in Mongolia.

Key terms, decoded

clan
A group of families who consider themselves related by descent from a common ancestor.
contiguous
Joined together in one unbroken stretch, with no gaps in between.
envoy
An official messenger sent to represent a ruler or state in dealings with another.
interregnum
A gap between one ruler's death and the next ruler taking power.
khan
A title for a ruler or chieftain among Mongol and Turkic peoples.
meritocracy
A system in which people rise according to their ability and results rather than their birth or connections.
posthumously deified
Declared to be a god after death.
shaman
A religious specialist believed to communicate with spirits, often through trance, to heal or to guide decisions.
steppe
A vast, flat, treeless grassland, of the kind that stretches across Central Asia.
tribal structure
A way of organising society in which loyalty and position come from which family group you were born into.
