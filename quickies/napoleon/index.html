Napoleon

History remembers him as tiny, but he was actually of average height. That was just one of the stories told about the boy bullied at school who crowned himself Emperor, then escaped exile to take France back.

3 min read · 10 key facts · 7 terms decoded

The Short Man Who Wasn't Short

Everyone "knows" Napoleon was tiny. It is one of history's most famous facts, and it is wrong. At about 168 centimetres, or 5 feet 6 inches, he was of average height for his time.

The myth came largely from his enemies. During the wars, the British press mocked him as a short-tempered little man, nicknamed "Little Boney". A nursery rhyme even warned children that he ate naughty people, turning him into a bogeyman.

His famous look adds to the legend. He is often shown in a large two-cornered hat worn sideways, with one hand tucked into his waistcoat. That pose comes from a painting made in 1812 by Jacques-Louis David.

The Bullied Boy From Corsica

Napoleon was born on 15 August 1769 on the island of Corsica, just a year after it became French. His family was of Italian origin, and his first languages were Corsican and Italian, not French.

At age 9, he was sent to school in mainland France. At military school, other students bullied him for his accent, his birthplace, his small build and his poor French. He became quiet and threw himself into books.

He was brilliant at maths. One examiner wrote that he "would make an excellent sailor". Instead, he trained as an artillery officer in Paris. When his father died, he had to finish a two-year course in one year, and he became the first Corsican to graduate from that school.

From Soldier to Emperor

Napoleon rose fast during the French Revolution. After winning key battles in the 1790s, he became a national hero. In 1799, he helped overthrow the government and made himself First Consul, the most powerful man in France.

In 1804, France declared him Emperor. At his grand ceremony in Paris on 2 December, he did something bold: he took the crown and placed it on his own head. Then he crowned his wife, Joséphine.

He won famous victories, including the Battle of Austerlitz in 1805, and came to dominate much of Europe. His expedition to Egypt even brought along 167 scientists, whose discoveries included the Rosetta Stone, the key that later unlocked ancient Egyptian writing.

The Great Escape From Elba

Napoleon's fall began with a disastrous invasion of Russia in 1812. In 1814, his enemies captured Paris and forced him to give up the throne. They sent him into exile on Elba, a small Mediterranean island, and even made him its ruler.

He did not stay long. On 26 February 1815, he slipped away with about 1,000 men. When a regiment was sent to stop him, he walked up to the soldiers alone and called out, "Here I am. Kill your Emperor, if you wish!" They cheered and joined him instead.

He marched into Paris and took back power. But his return, known as the Hundred Days, ended at the Battle of Waterloo in June 1815. He was sent to the remote island of Saint Helena, where he died in 1821, aged 51.

The Laws That Outlived Him

Napoleon's battles are famous, but his most lasting mark may be a book of laws. The Napoleonic Code, brought in in 1804, replaced a messy mix of local rules with one clear set of national laws.

It stood for equality before the law, secure property rights, and equal inheritance among children. It also had flaws, reducing the rights of women and children.

His armies carried the code across Europe, and it went on to shape the law in many parts of the world. Even his enemies admired his mind. The Duke of Wellington, who beat him at Waterloo, said his presence on a battlefield was worth 40,000 soldiers.

10 key facts

  1. At about 168 centimetres, Napoleon was of average height, not short.
  2. British newspapers nicknamed him "Little Boney".
  3. Napoleon's first languages were Corsican and Italian, not French.
  4. He was the first Corsican to graduate from the École militaire in Paris.
  5. At his 1804 ceremony, Napoleon placed the crown on his own head.
  6. His Egyptian expedition included 167 scientists, and their finds included the Rosetta Stone.
  7. He escaped from exile on Elba in 1815 with about 1,000 men.
  8. The Duke of Wellington said Napoleon's presence on the battlefield was worth 40,000 soldiers.
  9. In 1803, Napoleon sold the territory of Louisiana to the United States.
  10. Up to one million people lined the route of his funeral procession in Paris in 1840.

Key terms, decoded

artillery
Large, heavy guns such as cannons, and the soldiers trained to use them.
bogeyman
An imaginary scary figure used to frighten children into behaving.
exile
Being forced to leave your home country and live somewhere else.
First Consul
The top leader of France's government from 1799, a role that gave Napoleon most of the power in the country.
Hundred Days
The short period in 1815 between Napoleon's return from Elba and his final defeat at Waterloo.
Napoleonic Code
The set of civil laws created under Napoleon that became the model for legal systems in many countries.
Rosetta Stone
An ancient stone carved with the same text in three scripts, which helped experts learn to read Egyptian hieroglyphs.
