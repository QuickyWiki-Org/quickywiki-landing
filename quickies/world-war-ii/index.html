World War II

The deadliest conflict in human history, and most of the dead were civilians. It ended with two atomic bombs and built the international order we still live inside.

3 min read · 10 key facts · 14 terms decoded

Sixty to Seventy-Five Million People

World War II — 1 September 1939 to 2 September 1945 — was a global conflict between two coalitions, the Allies and the Axis powers. Nearly every country on Earth participated, many engaging in total war on an unprecedented scale.

It was the deadliest conflict in history, causing the deaths of 60 to 75 million people. The detail that matters most in that figure is who died: a majority were civilians.

Millions died from massacres, starvation, disease and genocides including the Holocaust. This was not a war whose casualties were mostly soldiers at fronts; it was a war conducted across populations. Its causes included unresolved tensions left over from the First World War, the rise of fascism in Europe and militarism in Japan.

It Started Before It Started

The conflict is dated from 1939, but the machinery was already running.

Key preceding events included Japan's invasion of Manchuria in 1931, the Spanish Civil War, the outbreak of the Second Sino-Japanese War in 1937, and Germany's annexations of Austria and the Sudetenland.

The formal beginning came on 1 September 1939, when Nazi Germany under Adolf Hitler invaded Poland, after which the United Kingdom and France declared war. Poland was also invaded by the Soviet Union in mid-September and partitioned between the two states under the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact. In 1940 the Soviet Union annexed the Baltic states and parts of Finland and Romania, while Germany conquered Norway, Denmark, Belgium, Luxembourg and the Netherlands. After the fall of France in June 1940, the war continued mainly between Germany, now assisted by Fascist Italy, and the British Empire and Commonwealth — through the Balkans, the Mediterranean, the Battle of Britain, the Blitz and the Battle of the Atlantic.

The Year It Turned

In June 1941 Germany invaded the Soviet Union, opening the Eastern Front. In December 1941 Japan attacked American and British territories across Asia and the Pacific, including Pearl Harbor, bringing the United States into the war.

Then the momentum reversed, and it happened in roughly eighteen months. Japan's advances were halted in June 1942 at the Battle of Midway. In early 1943, Axis forces were defeated in North Africa and at Stalingrad.

An Allied invasion of Italy in July 1943 brought down its fascist regime, and offensives in the Pacific and by the Soviet Union forced the Axis into retreat on all fronts. In 1944 the Western Allies landed in France at Normandy, opening a new front, while the Soviet Union advanced into Central Europe. Japan lost key Western Pacific islands, saw its navy crippled, and was defeated in Burma.

Two Bombs, and a New World Order

The war in Europe ended with the liberation of occupied territories, the invasion of Germany, the fall of Berlin, and unconditional surrender on 8 May 1945.

In the Pacific it ended differently. On 6 and 9 August the US dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki, followed by a Soviet invasion of Japanese-occupied Manchuria. Japan announced unconditional surrender on 15 August and signed the surrender document on 2 September 1945. Afterwards Germany, Austria, Japan and Korea were occupied, and German and Japanese leaders were tried for war crimes.

The aftermath built the world that followed. The United Nations was created to foster cooperation and prevent future conflicts, with the victorious great powers — China, France, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom and the United States — becoming the permanent members of its Security Council. The Soviet Union and the United States emerged as rival superpowers, setting up the Cold War, while the waning influence of Europe's great powers triggered the decolonisation of Africa and Asia.

10 key facts

  1. World War II caused the deaths of 60 to 75 million people, the majority of them civilians.
  2. Millions died from massacres, starvation, disease and genocides including the Holocaust.
  3. Key events preceding the war included Japan's 1931 invasion of Manchuria and the 1937 outbreak of the Second Sino-Japanese War.
  4. Poland was invaded by Germany on 1 September 1939 and by the Soviet Union in mid-September, then partitioned between them.
  5. Japan's Pacific advance was halted at the Battle of Midway in June 1942.
  6. Axis forces were defeated in North Africa and at Stalingrad in early 1943.
  7. The Western Allies opened a new front by invading France at Normandy in 1944.
  8. Germany surrendered unconditionally on 8 May 1945 after the fall of Berlin.
  9. The United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki on 6 and 9 August 1945.
  10. The United Nations was created afterwards, with the five victorious great powers becoming permanent Security Council members.

Key terms, decoded

Allies
The coalition opposing the Axis, including Britain, the Soviet Union, the United States and China.
annexation
One state taking control of another's territory and declaring it its own.
Axis powers
The coalition led by Germany, Italy and Japan.
decolonisation
The process by which colonies gained independence from European empires.
Eastern Front
The vast theatre of war between Germany and the Soviet Union, the deadliest of the conflict.
fascism
An authoritarian, ultra-nationalist political system built on a single leader and suppression of opposition.
genocide
The deliberate attempt to destroy a national, ethnic, racial or religious group.
militarism
The belief that a state should maintain strong armed forces and use them assertively.
Security Council
The United Nations body responsible for international peace and security, with five permanent members.
superpower
A state with enough military, economic and political reach to influence events worldwide.
the Holocaust
The systematic murder of six million Jews and millions of others by Nazi Germany and its collaborators.
total war
A conflict in which a country mobilises its entire population and economy, and civilian targets are included.
unconditional surrender
Giving up entirely, without negotiating any terms.
war crime
A serious violation of the laws of war, such as deliberately targeting civilians or prisoners.
