Heart

It is about the size of your closed fist, beats around 72 times a minute without being told to, and its failure kills more people than anything else on Earth.

3 min read · 10 key facts · 18 terms decoded

A Fist-Sized Pump

The heart is a muscular organ found in humans and other animals, which pumps blood through the blood vessels. Together they make up the circulatory system.

The job is delivery and collection at once: the pumped blood carries oxygen and nutrients to the tissues, while carrying metabolic waste such as carbon dioxide to the lungs.

In humans it is approximately the size of a closed fist, sitting between the lungs in the middle compartment of the chest, called the mediastinum. It is divided into four chambers — the upper left and right atria, and the lower left and right ventricles. The right atrium and ventricle are usually referred to together as the right heart, and their left counterparts as the left heart. It is enclosed in a protective sac, the pericardium, and its wall has three layers: epicardium, myocardium and endocardium.

It Generates Its Own Signal

The most remarkable thing about the heart is that nothing tells it to beat.

It pumps with a rhythm determined by a group of pacemaker cells in the sinoatrial node. These generate an electric current that causes the heart to contract, travelling through the atrioventricular node and along the heart's own conduction system.

The heart contains its own electrical generator and its own wiring. The brain can speed it up or slow it down, but the beat originates inside the muscle itself — which is why a heart can continue beating outside the body under the right conditions, and why the organ is described as having its own rhythm rather than receiving one. In adults, the resting rate is close to 72 beats per minute. Blood flows one way only, because heart valves prevent backflow.

The Route Blood Takes

The circuit runs in a figure of eight, and it passes through the heart twice.

Deoxygenated blood enters the right atrium from the superior and inferior venae cavae, and passes to the right ventricle. From there it is pumped into the pulmonary circulation to the lungs, where it takes on oxygen and gives off carbon dioxide.

Oxygenated blood then returns to the left atrium, passes through the left ventricle, and is pumped out through the aorta into the systemic circulation. It travels through arteries, then arterioles, then capillaries — where nutrients and other substances are exchanged between vessels and cells — before returning through venules and veins to start again. That is why the left side is more muscular: it pushes blood around the entire body, while the right side only has to reach the lungs.

The Leading Cause of Death

Cardiovascular diseases are the most common cause of death globally. An estimated 19.8 million people died from them in 2022 — about 32% of all deaths worldwide. Of those, 85% were due to heart attack and stroke.

The risk factors are largely known and largely shared: smoking, being overweight, little exercise, high cholesterol, high blood pressure, and poorly controlled diabetes, among others.

The difficult part is detection. Cardiovascular diseases do not frequently have symptoms, though they may cause chest pain or shortness of breath. Diagnosis is often made by taking a medical history, listening to heart sounds with a stethoscope, and using an ECG or an echocardiogram, which uses ultrasound. Specialists in the heart are called cardiologists, though many medical specialties may be involved in treatment.

10 key facts

  1. The human heart is roughly the size of a closed fist and sits between the lungs in the middle of the chest.
  2. It has four chambers: left and right atria above, left and right ventricles below.
  3. The heartbeat is generated inside the heart itself, by pacemaker cells in the sinoatrial node.
  4. The adult resting heart rate is close to 72 beats per minute.
  5. Heart valves ensure blood can only flow one way through the organ.
  6. Blood passes through the heart twice per circuit: once to the lungs and once to the rest of the body.
  7. The left side of the heart is more muscular because it pumps blood to the whole body, not just the lungs.
  8. Cardiovascular diseases are the most common cause of death worldwide.
  9. An estimated 19.8 million people died of cardiovascular disease in 2022, about 32% of all deaths.
  10. 85% of those deaths were due to heart attack and stroke.

Key terms, decoded

aorta
The largest artery in the body, carrying blood out of the heart to the systemic circulation.
atrioventricular node
The relay point that passes the electrical signal from the upper chambers to the lower ones.
atrium
One of the two upper chambers of the heart, which receive blood arriving from the body or lungs.
capillary
The smallest blood vessel, thin enough for oxygen and nutrients to pass into surrounding tissue.
cardiologist
A doctor who specialises in the heart and its diseases.
circulatory system
The heart and blood vessels together, which move blood around the body.
echocardiogram
An ultrasound scan that produces a moving image of the heart.
electrocardiogram
A test that records the heart's electrical activity through electrodes on the skin.
heart valve
A flap inside the heart that opens one way only, preventing blood from flowing backwards.
mediastinum
The central compartment of the chest between the lungs, where the heart sits.
myocardium
The thick muscular middle layer of the heart wall that does the actual pumping.
pacemaker cell
A specialised heart cell that generates the electrical impulse triggering each beat.
pericardium
The protective sac surrounding the heart, containing a small amount of fluid.
pulmonary circulation
The loop carrying blood from the heart to the lungs and back.
sinoatrial node
The small cluster of cells in the right atrium that sets the heart's natural rhythm.
systemic circulation
The loop carrying oxygenated blood from the heart to the rest of the body.
vena cava
One of the two large veins returning oxygen-poor blood from the body to the heart.
ventricle
One of the two lower chambers of the heart, which pump blood out.
